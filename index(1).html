SWAT+ · Hydrology · GIS · Rwanda · 2026

Sebeya catchment: can paying farmers keep the rivers clear?

A SWAT+ model of a steep 365 km² volcanic catchment, built from a 10 m DEM and a purpose-made land-cover map, calibrated at the Nyundo gauge and tested under 16 climate futures. It estimates how terraces and river buffers paid for under a PES scheme change sediment, flood peaks and dry-season flow at two hydropower plants and two water-supply intakes. For Rwanda’s Ministry of Environment and Water Resources Board, with GGGI.

PES · Rwanda · 2025–present · GGGI

Rwanda PES: pre-feasibility and scheme design

Design of a Payment for Ecosystem Services scheme to reward sustainable natural-resource management and restore Rwanda’s wetlands and catchments, starting with the Rubavu and Rutsiro districts.

The challenge

Rwanda’s landscapes are under pressure from erosion and land-use change, yet the communities best placed to protect them see little direct reward for doing so.

My role

Scheme design end-to-end: the governance framework, stakeholder engagement with government and private actors, and the pre-feasibility study — ecosystem-service valuation and cost–benefit analysis — defended to government counterparts. Blended-finance pathways and gender and social inclusion are built into the design. The Sebeya SWAT+ modelling provides the biophysical evidence for the feasibility stage.

PES · India · Narmada landscape · GGGI

Narmada forest-fire PES model

A first-of-its-kind Payment for Ecosystem Services model that pays communities to reduce forest-fire risk across the Narmada landscape, alongside a “forest-friendly village” community-forestry initiative and grassland-management work.

The challenge

Recurrent forest fires degraded the Narmada watershed, but communities had no incentive to invest the effort needed to prevent them.

My role

Helped develop the PES model, led the community-forestry initiative, ran NTFP value-chain analysis across two districts and supported grassland management. Presented the project at World Water Week 2024 in Stockholm.

Bioeconomy · Rwanda · 2025–present · GGGI

Rwanda forest bioeconomy

Project concepts and a bioeconomy programme for the western region of Rwanda, in partnership with the Government of Denmark — identifying value chains that turn biological resources into local income while restoring landscapes.

The opportunity

Western Rwanda’s forests and biological resources hold real potential for value-added products and jobs, if the value chains and enabling finance can be built around them.

My role

Concept and programme design, drawing on bioeconomy and value-chain work from India to shape something suited to Rwanda’s context.

Value chains · India · Narmada / Indore · GGGI

NTFP value chains and business incubation

Value chains for forest-based products — lac resin and leaf plates pressed from Butea monosperma (Palash) — with business incubation to help community enterprises reach urban markets, alongside a carbon-based incentive model developed with advocacy agencies and private-sector partners.

The challenge

Forest communities harvested valuable non-timber produce but captured little of its market value, with no route from raw resource to finished, sellable product.

My role

Mapped the value chains, identified where value was leaking, incubated community enterprises and built the market linkages that connected producers to urban demand.

Feasibility · India · Sidhi District

Forest bio-economy feasibility

Scoping and feasibility assessment of forest bio-economy opportunities in Sidhi District, evaluating biomass and NTFP value chains for viable business models, paired with restoration activity.

The challenge

A forest-dependent district with abundant biomass and forest-derived residues, but few viable business models to turn them into local income.

My role

Assessed biomass and forest-derived waste opportunities and integrated them into district planning and restoration, coordinating with WRI and district departments.
