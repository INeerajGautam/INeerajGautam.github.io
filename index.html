01 · About

An economist who works where ecology meets livelihoods.

I'm an environmental economist who fell for the idea that nature could be protected not by fencing people out, but by making conservation worth their while.

My path started in an unlikely place — an electrical engineering degree. But somewhere between circuits and systems, I became far more curious about forests, rivers and the communities who depend on them. That curiosity carried me into an MBA in Conservation Management, and then into the field.

For seven years I've worked at the meeting point of ecology, economics and people — from the teak forests of Madhya Pradesh, where I helped design a first-of-its-kind scheme paying villages to prevent forest fires, to the hills of Rwanda, where I now design Payment for Ecosystem Services schemes for wetland and catchment restoration.

I've spent long days walking value chains with forest communities, sitting in district offices negotiating restoration plans, and long nights building the InVEST sediment models and cost-benefit analyses that turn those conversations into numbers a government can fund. I care about the whole arc: the science that proves an ecosystem's worth, the economics that makes protecting it viable, and the people whose livelihoods have to come out ahead.

What keeps me going is a simple conviction — that the fairest climate solutions are the ones where the community closest to the land also benefits most from keeping it healthy.

Gautam (noun)

An economist who counts what usually goes uncounted — the value of a standing forest, a clean watershed, a community's stake in the land — and turns it into a reason to protect them.

Rwanda PES Scheme Design

Leading the design of a Payment for Ecosystem Services scheme to reward sustainable natural-resource management and restore Rwanda's wetlands and catchments.

upload images/case-rwanda.jpg (map, model output or field photo)

The challenge

Rwanda's landscapes are under pressure from erosion and land-use change, yet the communities best placed to protect them see little direct reward for doing so.

My role

I lead the scheme design end-to-end — the governance framework, stakeholder engagement with government and private actors, and the pre-feasibility study, including ecosystem-service valuation and cost-benefit analysis.

Approach

Applying lessons from earlier incentive-based work in India, combined with spatial analysis and InVEST modelling to identify where payments deliver the greatest ecological return.

Narmada Forest-Fire PES Model

A first-of-its-kind Payment for Ecosystem Services model that pays communities to reduce forest-fire risk across the Narmada landscape.

upload images/case-narmada.jpg

The challenge

Recurrent forest fires degraded the Narmada watershed, but communities had no incentive to invest the effort needed to prevent them.

My role

I helped develop the PES model and led the "forest-friendly village" community-forestry initiative, coordinating with district authorities and presenting at water-governance roundtables.

Outcome

A working incentive model linking fire-risk reduction to payments — a template other landscapes can adapt.
