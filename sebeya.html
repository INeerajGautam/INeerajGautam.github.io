Keeping the Sebeya clear

A SWAT+ model of a steep volcanic catchment, built from the DEM up, calibrated on three kinds of evidence and stress-tested under 16 climate futures, to see whether paying farmers for soil conservation would keep sediment out of two hydropower plants and two drinking-water intakes.

1GIS: terrain and land cover

I used the 10 m national DEM because it resolves the short, steep slopes where terraces matter. A block of artefacts was patched from a 30 m DEM, and the lower river network was burnt in so flow routes correctly to Lake Kivu. An independent priority-flood/D8 script confirmed the routing before the QSWAT+ build: all five outlets fall within 1% of their reference areas.

Global land-cover products disagreed, so I labelled a 400-point stratified reference sample from high-resolution imagery. ESA WorldCover turned out to miss about half the cropland. A Random Forest on Sentinel-2 seasonal composites, Dynamic World probabilities and building footprints produced the model's 10 m map.

Accuracy method. Stratified estimator (Olofsson et al. 2014; Stehman 2014) with spatially blocked cross-validation (2 km blocks). Class areas come from the sample with 95% confidence intervals; cropland 13,224 ± 1,252 ha. HRUs. Land use × six soil units × five slope bands, so measures can be assigned by slope class. I reproduced the QSWAT+ filtering offline to choose thresholds that kept every class within 0.5% of its mapped area.

2Calibration

The first runs looked plausible but used the wrong rain. Reading diagnostics.out and the SWAT+ source, I found that rain stations are looked up by binary search, so an unsorted station list silently replaced three gauges with the weather generator over 98% of the catchment. After the fix, each parameter was set from a measurable process and checked against the Nyundo gauge, 2025 telemetry and hydropower plant flows.

Also from the source code. In rev 62 the precipitation lapse rate (plaps) is added on every day, including dry days, so it was left at zero. Plant flows as a lower bound. The model now drops below Keya's plant flow on 45 of 318 days in 2024 (default model: 267), and never below Gihira's in 24 months.

3Erosion and where the measures go

Hillslope erosion comes from MUSLE. Because SWAT+ under-represents bank and channel sources, I added a near-channel component (seasonal concentration × flow). Hillslopes then supply about 40% of the load at the intake, consistent with the 30–45% found by sediment fingerprinting in the Mugogo catchment.

Terraces enter through the USLE P factor measured on Rwandan runoff plots (bench 0.02, progressive 0.30) and a lower curve number; slope length is left unchanged because the plot values already include it. Buffers act as filter strips and cut near-channel sediment.

Targeted package (TS13). Terraces on class IV cropland (40–60% slope) and the most erosive class III fields, plus 10 m buffers on 309 km of river. Business as usual to 2055. Existing terraces (EWMR survey: 25% bench, 11% progressive) degrade without maintenance; built-up land grows about 115 ha a year.

4Scenario results

Four packages built from the 2018 Catchment Plan's slope rules were run over 30 years of observed weather (1996–2025) against business as usual. Targeting the steepest cropland saves about twice as much soil per hectare as the full programme and delivers more than 80% of its benefit on 37% of the area.

All results: Gihira treatment works, central sediment case, mature measures against the BAU path, mean of years 5–30.Forest trade-off. Native forest on slopes over 60% raises ET by about 150 mm/yr and cuts recharge, so the full programme lowers dry-season flow (Q95) by about 3%.

5Climate change: CMIP6 ensemble

Daily NASA NEX-GDDP-CMIP6 data (0.25°) for eight models under SSP2-4.5 and SSP5-8.5 were transferred to the 1996–2025 record by monthly quantile perturbation, so heavy-rain days change more than light ones where the model says so, and run with 2055 land use. The models disagree on wetter or drier, but the PES effect is steady: 36–40% less sediment in every future, and below today's load in 15 of 16.

Method. Ntegeka et al. (2014); 2031–2060 against 1985–2014. The eight models were checked against all 33 for rainfall change and warming.Annual flood peaks rise by about 50% at the median, which PES cannot offset. CO₂ effects on plant water use are not included.

6Uncertainty

Five alternative parameter sets varied baseflow, storm runoff, interflow, ET and the volcanic aquifer; two were rejected because they did worse than the mean flow on telemetry (NSE −1.12 and −0.61). Three assumptions about the riverbank share of sediment were crossed with the parameters and the climate futures. The order of the packages never changes.

Next step. Paired treated and untreated sub-catchments with turbidity loggers, set up before payments start, would test the bank-erosion assumption directly.
